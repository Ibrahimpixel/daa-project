    How the two halves talk

    The first algorithm’s output is the second one’s input. That’s the whole trick.

    Feed in hosts, links, budget, patches and windows.
    Kruskal + DSU finds the cheapest links to secure. Already-secure links cost 0 and go first.
    Grow coverage from the SOC, cheapest affordable link at a time, until the budget runs dry.
    Hosts with no secured path are flagged exposed. They get zero patches.
    Everything else enters the pool. Knapsack fills window 1, those patches leave, window 2 goes next.
    Dashboard shows spend, coverage, patch schedule and risk mitigated.

    What it costs to run

    V hosts, E links, n patches, W window hours, T windows.

    PartTimeSpace
    Sort linksO(E log E)O(E)
    DSU operationsO(E α(V))O(V)
    Kruskal totalO(E log E)O(V + E)
    SOC expansion (our version)O(V · E)O(V + E)
    Knapsack, per windowO(n · W)O(n · W)
    Whole pipelineO(E log E + V·E + T·n·W)O(V + E + n·W)

    Stuff we admit up front

    An MST minimises hardening cost. It does not model attackers moving sideways through the network.
    The SOC-rooted expansion is a heuristic. The budgeted version of the problem is NP-hard.
    Several windows at once is the multiple knapsack problem (strongly NP-hard), so filling windows one after another isn’t guaranteed globally optimal.
    All data is synthetic and the risk numbers are illustrative. Real CVSS scores are future work.
